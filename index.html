Past Academic Research

Biodiversity and carbon across scale, space, and climate.

My academic research asks how biodiversity responds to human impacts across scales: from the local field plots where organisms are counted, to the broad landscapes where conservation and climate decisions are made. Much of this work combines tropical field biology, bird-community data, Bayesian and hierarchical models, and careful attention to what is lost when ecological variation is collapsed into a single number.

Across projects, the common thread is measurement under complexity: land-use change in tropical forests, climate-linked shifts in bird populations, beta and zeta diversity, macro-demography, occupancy models, and the statistical infrastructure needed to turn messy biodiversity observations into defensible evidence. For a complete publication list, see my Google Scholar profile.
